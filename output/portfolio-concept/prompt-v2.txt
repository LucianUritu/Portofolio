Use case: ui-mockup.
Create a NEW high-fidelity website design image for Lucian Uritu's personal software engineering portfolio. The client rejected the previous design because it looked too AI generated and focused too much on a single project. This version must be much simpler, natural, understated, and reflect his WHOLE CV. Design a beautiful usable website, not a decorated CV document or poster.

OUTPUT: one flat front-facing full-page desktop website screenshot, portrait aspect ratio approximately 2:3, ideally 1800 x 2700. Crisp readable type. No browser frame, device, mockup environment, annotations, watermark, decorative objects, or presentation board.

ART DIRECTION: white canvas, charcoal typography, a single quiet medium-blue accent, subtle light grey surface only where useful. More like a thoughtful independent engineer's personal site than a design agency, SaaS landing page, or futuristic tech demo. One sans-serif family resembling Switzer, with excellent typographic spacing and hierarchy. White #FFFFFF, charcoal #22272B, secondary slate #616A74, blue #315ECA, pale grey #F2F4F6. Every headline is one color and one consistent type treatment. Calm regular and medium weights, no oversized heavy slogan, no gradients, no 3D renders, no traffic pictures, no illustrations, no stock photos, no fake app screenshots, no icons in colored squares, no glass, no floating cards, no pills, no badges, no decorative grids, no arbitrary numbered labels, no thick rules. Design interest comes from alignment, hierarchy, balance, and thoughtful spacing. Thin rules should be avoided; use whitespace and light surface changes. The website looks deliberately simple and real.

FRAME: a centered content width of about 1150px within an 1800px image, so generous equal margins. One consistent left alignment for section titles. Section headings in a narrow left column about 20% of content width, section content in a wide right column about 75%, except hero and projects which use full width. This repeated alignment makes a coherent website. Keep useful detail and avoid huge empty stretches.
